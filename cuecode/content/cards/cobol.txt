%%card cb-pic-pad
%%deck cobol
%%kind predict
%%ask What does this print? (Mind the spaces.)
%%code cobol
       01  WS-ITEM  PIC X(5)  VALUE "AB".
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           DISPLAY "[" WS-ITEM "]".
%%answer
[AB   ]
%%why
`PIC X(5)` is always 5 characters, so it's padded with spaces on the right.

%%card cb-truncate-text
%%deck cobol
%%kind predict
%%ask What does this print?
%%code cobol
       01  WS-CODE  PIC X(3).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           MOVE "ABCDEF" TO WS-CODE.
           DISPLAY WS-CODE.
%%answer
ABC
%%why
A `MOVE` to a smaller text field cuts off the right side, with no warning.

%%card cb-truncate-number
%%deck cobol
%%kind predict
%%ask What does this print?
%%code cobol
       01  WS-N  PIC 9(2).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           MOVE 1234 TO WS-N.
           DISPLAY WS-N.
%%answer
34
%%why
A `MOVE` to a smaller number field cuts off the left side, the big digits. Silent data loss, and a classic bug.

%%card cb-implied-decimal
%%deck cobol
%%kind predict
%%ask What does this print?
%%code cobol
       01  WS-PRICE  PIC 9(3)V99  VALUE 12.50.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           DISPLAY WS-PRICE.
%%answer
01250
%%why
`V` is an invisible decimal point. The field stores 5 digits. You need an edited field like `ZZ9.99` to show the dot.

%%card cb-edited
%%deck cobol
%%kind predict
%%ask What does this print?
%%code cobol
       01  WS-PRICE  PIC 9(3)V99  VALUE 12.50.
       01  WS-OUT    PIC ZZ9.99.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           MOVE WS-PRICE TO WS-OUT.
           DISPLAY "[" WS-OUT "]".
%%answer
[ 12.50]
%%why
`Z` turns a leading zero into a space, and the `.` is a real printed dot.

%%card cb-table-one
%%deck cobol
%%kind predict
%%ask Where do tables start? What does this print?
%%code cobol
       01  WS-T.
           05  WS-CELL  PIC X  OCCURS 3 TIMES.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           MOVE "A" TO WS-CELL(1).
           MOVE "B" TO WS-CELL(2).
           MOVE "C" TO WS-CELL(3).
           DISPLAY WS-CELL(2).
%%answer
B
%%why
COBOL tables start at 1, so `(2)` is the second item. In most languages it would be `[1]`.

%%card cb-rounded
%%deck cobol
%%kind predict
%%ask What does this print?
%%code cobol
       01  WS-A  PIC 9(2)V9  VALUE 1.5.
       01  WS-B  PIC 9(2).
       01  WS-C  PIC 9(2).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           COMPUTE WS-B = WS-A.
           COMPUTE WS-C ROUNDED = WS-A.
           DISPLAY WS-B " " WS-C.
%%answer
01 02
%%why
Without `ROUNDED`, COBOL cuts off the extra digits. With it, 1.5 rounds up to 2.

%%card cb-perform-until
%%deck cobol
%%kind predict
%%ask How many times does the body run?
%%code cobol
       01  WS-N  PIC 9(2)  VALUE 5.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           PERFORM UNTIL WS-N >= 5
               DISPLAY "HI"
           END-PERFORM.
           DISPLAY "DONE".
%%answer
DONE
%%why
`PERFORM UNTIL` checks before it starts. The answer is zero times. `WITH TEST AFTER` would run it once.

%%card cb-move-to
%%deck cobol
%%kind fill
%%ask Fill the gap to copy a value into a field.
%%code cobol
       01  WS-NAME  PIC X(10).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           ____ "ASHA" TO WS-NAME.
           DISPLAY WS-NAME.
%%answer
MOVE
%%why
`MOVE` is COBOL's assignment. It reads source first, then target.

%%card cb-end-if
%%deck cobol
%%kind fill
%%ask Fill the gap to close the `IF` properly.
%%code cobol
       01  WS-N  PIC 9  VALUE 1.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           IF WS-N = 1
               DISPLAY "ONE"
           ____.
%%answer
END-IF
%%why
Close with `END-IF`. Don't rely on a full stop, because it ends every open `IF` at once.

%%card cb-write-loop
%%deck cobol
%%kind write
%%ask Write a loop that shows the numbers 1 to 3 using `PERFORM VARYING`.
%%code cobol
       01  WS-I  PIC 9(2).
%%answer cobol
       01  WS-I  PIC 9(2).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           PERFORM VARYING WS-I FROM 1 BY 1 UNTIL WS-I > 3
               DISPLAY WS-I
           END-PERFORM.
%%why
`VARYING` sets the start, the step and the stop condition.

%%card cb-divisions
%%deck cobol
%%kind explain
%%ask Name the four divisions of a COBOL program, in order.
%%answer
IDENTIFICATION (its name), ENVIRONMENT (files and machine), DATA (fields), PROCEDURE (the logic).

%%card cb-columns
%%deck cobol
%%kind explain
%%ask In fixed-format COBOL, what are columns 7, 8 and 12, and why does 72 matter?
%%answer
Column 7 marks comments with `*`. Area A starts at column 8, for divisions, paragraphs and 01 levels. Area B starts at column 12, for statements. Anything past column 72 is ignored.

%%card cb-fix-loop
%%deck cobol
%%kind fix
%%ask This loop never stops. Fix it.
%%code cobol
       01  WS-N  PIC 9.
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           PERFORM VARYING WS-N FROM 1 BY 1 UNTIL WS-N > 9
               DISPLAY WS-N
           END-PERFORM.
%%answer cobol
       01  WS-N  PIC 9(2).
       PROCEDURE DIVISION.
           PERFORM VARYING WS-N FROM 1 BY 1 UNTIL WS-N > 9
               DISPLAY WS-N
           END-PERFORM.
%%why
`PIC 9` holds 0 to 9. Adding 1 to 9 wraps to 0, so it never gets past 9. Give the counter room.
