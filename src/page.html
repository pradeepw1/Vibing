Good to know

What no browser hides

  • Logging in. Once you sign in to a site, it knows it’s you, whatever browser you use.
  • Your internet provider can usually see which websites you connect to (not the pages inside them), unless you use a VPN or iCloud Private Relay.
  • Private and Incognito windows only stop history being saved on your device. Sites, trackers and your internet provider can still see you.
  • Your search engine matters as much as your browser. Safari and Chrome both send your searches to Google unless you change it.

How the grades work

  • Every grade is about what happens with the settings the browser comes with. Most of the “Exposed” items can be changed; the details say how.
  • Good means it’s blocked, it’s off until you turn it on, or nothing tied to you leaves. Partial means some data goes out but it’s limited or anonymous, or protection only covers part of the problem. Exposed means it’s sent or left open by default.
  • What companies say they do is taken from their own pages; we can’t audit their servers. Where a fact comes only from user reports, the details say so.
  • Versions: Chrome and Brave current releases, Safari 27 (September 2026), DuckDuckGo current apps.
